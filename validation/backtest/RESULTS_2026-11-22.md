## 5. What the passes are worth

- **Two comparisons were scored out of twelve.** Under §7 a channel needs three scored sites, so no grade could change whatever the results were. No misses is not evidence that the engine is right: ten of twelve comparisons were never tested.
- **Grant's rate pass is weak.** The pass interval is 28 points wide (−20.0 to +7.9 pp), because the two observed counterfactuals disagree by 18 points (§6.3). Every allocation and both ownership cases landed between −1.4% and +0.5% (p50). Across seeds the result tracks one assumed input, the margin kept on the site's sales (Spearman −0.91 to −0.98), as in D-101.
- **Wasco's water pass is conditional on two inputs taken from the record:** the load (NWCPUD's whole sales increment over 2002–04, which includes any non-Google growth) and evaporative cooling (Google's own page). The engine's water intensity, about 1.7 L per IT kWh at p50 in 2021, is what was tested. A pass means water per unit of load was right in scale (ratios 0.81–1.20, all ten years within 0.5–2.0), not that the engine predicted The Dalles' water demand. Predictions run low in 9 of 10 years (2018 is the exception).
- **The grid check was not scored at either site,** and its recorded values would carry little even if it had been. The mask removed 8 substations at Wasco, BPA's Chenoweth among them, and 4 at Grant, because none was dated before T0 (D-203). With them gone, the engine called for a new substation and a new ≥69 kV line in 100% of seeds at both sites. That is what the mask would produce regardless of what was built.

- **Scorer bug, fixed before any result was read.** The first `score_run.py` invocation stopped with a TypeError before printing or writing anything. PyYAML reads `104.3e6` in `sites.yaml` as a string, because YAML 1.1 needs a sign in the exponent. The fix converts the value to a number (`float()`), and no rule changed. It is in the same commit as these results.
