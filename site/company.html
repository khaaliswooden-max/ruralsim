Test it here before it’s built there.

Most rural infrastructure is designed somewhere else and tested only after it’s been built. RuralSim moves that testing first, onto real counties rebuilt from public data, and scores every plan by what it does for the people who live there.

Mission

A test range for rural America

66.3 million Americans live in rural places, 20% of the country. 719 counties have no hospital at all. Decisions about their power, water, care and connectivity are often made with averages that hide who gets left out.

RuralSim is a reproducible digital twin: five pilot counties in 3D, a statistical table for every U.S. county, and ten experiments. Each experiment states its pass bar before it runs, and it fails if the worst-off tenth of households does not gain.

Thelvanor

Thelvanor builds. RuralSim proves.

Thelvanor builds small, solar-powered AI infrastructure for rural communities, anchored at hospitals, schools, courthouses and fire and police stations. RuralSim tests each design before anything is installed.

  • 20 MW data center on a rural co-op feeder. Lowest-income power rates fell 23.8% when it bought from the grid and rose 16.7% when it generated its own power. Same load, opposite outcome.
  • Hospital and school microgrid. Carried a 72-hour outage in 30 of 30 simulated winters in Teton County, Idaho.
  • Edge AI triage at the clinic. 84.8% uptime against a 99% target in Thurston County, Nebraska. With only 4 hours of battery, it shows what to fix next.

Simulated, screening-grade results: reduced-order models with assumed device parameters. Decision support, not a finding.

What we commit to

Reproducible

Methods, assumptions, data sources and limits are documented. A clean-machine rebuild reproduced all five county worlds, all 200 experiment runs bit for bit, and the national table byte for byte.

Open to the people it models

The models, their public data and their results are published here at no charge, so rural decision-makers can weigh options without paying for proprietary analysis.

Nonpartisan

Political and election variables are excluded in code from every objective and optimizer. RuralSim does not advocate for any party or candidate.
