Five rural counties, rebuilt to test what would help.

Each one is a 3D model built from public records: roads, buildings, terrain, power lines, water systems, hospitals and bridges. We use them to try ideas before anyone spends money on them.

The five counties

Each was chosen by a written rule to represent a different kind of rural America. Open one to fly over it, switch data layers on and off, and see where every layer came from.

What the experiments found

Ten ideas, each run 20 times on simulated households, each judged against a success line written down before it ran. Failures are reported as plainly as wins.

How to read this

These are screening results, not findings. The simulations are simplified models with assumed equipment numbers. They are good for deciding which ideas deserve a closer look, and not for making claims about real people.

Every household in the experiments is synthetic. No real person's records are used. Political and election data are excluded from every model.

Some layers are incomplete in ways we report rather than patch. OpenStreetMap misses many rural buildings, and federal water-system records have no locations, so some systems are counted but not placed on the map.

Data: U.S. Census Bureau, USDA ERS, CMS, USGS 3DEP and NHD, EPA SDWIS, FHWA National Bridge Inventory, EIA, BTS, USGS PAD-US, and Open-Meteo. Code and full provenance are on GitHub.
