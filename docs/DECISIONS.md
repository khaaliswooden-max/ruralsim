## D-018 (2026-11-11, Tier B pilot 2) — overpass.osm.ch mirror is corrupt; empty answers are data poison
The osm.ch Overpass mirror returns HTTP 200 with zero elements and a bogus timestamp for bboxes known to contain data — an empty answer is worse than a loud failure because it looks like "no features". It is removed from the endpoint list (private.coffee + kumi.systems only, 5-attempt backoff). Waterways queries that still fail write an empty layer by design: NHD is the manifest hydrology source. **General rule now encoded in fetchers: a fetched layer that is unexpectedly empty must be treated as suspect, not as ground truth.**

## D-019 (2026-11-11, Tier B pilot 2) — /mnt mount intermittently drops freshly written files
Four files across two builds vanished from `/mnt` after successful writes (siblings written seconds apart survived). `build_county.py` now declares expected payload globs per step (`EXPECT`), verifies after every step, retries the step up to 3×, and calls `os.sync()`. County-file globs are FIPS-specific so a surviving state-level sibling (`eia860_plants_ID.csv`) cannot mask a dropped county file.

*Refinement (2026-11-12, pilot 3):* drops are nondeterministic even with zero concurrent writers — a verified write can vanish within ~8 s and need multiple rewrites before it sticks (observed on `eia860_plants_54047.csv`, a header-only zero-plant CSV). Concurrent writers to the same path make drops near-certain, so: one build at a time per county, fetchers self-heal (verify + rewrite up to 5x before exit), and build_county's EXPECT check remains the final net.

## D-020 (2026-11-11, Tier B pilot 2) — Generalization bugs found by pilot 2 (fixed at the source)
Two pilot-1 constants survived parameterization and were caught only because pilot 2 rendered wrong: (a) the viewer terrain axis projection used hardcoded Thurston reference lon/lat (−96.5, 42.0), shifting Teton's grid +104 km in ENU-y so the camera framed an empty origin; (b) the DEM drape fallback defaulted to 340 m (Thurston elevation), which would bury unplaceable Teton overlays ~1.2 km under the terrain — it now samples the county centroid. **Rule: every per-county constant lives in `county_config.py` or is derived from the payload; a literal number in a build script is a bug.** Viewer template also had a use-before-declaration (`EXAG` referencing `terrMin/terrMax` before the `let`) that crashed the whole scene script; fixed by reordering.

## D-021 (2026-11-11, Tier B pilot 3) — Terrain fallback to 3DEP 1 arc-second via Planetary Computer under egress throttle
Sandbox egress dropped to ~15 KB/s globally on 2026-11-11 evening; TNM whole-tile downloads (~400 MB each) would take ~7 h per tile. **Fallback:** `fetch_terrain.py` probes sustained throughput after 90 s; below 45 KB/s it aborts and window-reads USGS 3DEP 1 arc-second (~30 m) COGs from the Planetary Computer `3dep-seamless` collection — same USGS program, same clip, payload stays at native ~30 m (`dem_<fips>_30m.tif`), source CRS EPSG:4269 treated as 4326 (<2 m datum shift, below the pixel). Partial TNM downloads write to `.part` and are renamed on completion, so an interrupted download can never masquerade as a complete tile. Counties built on the fallback say so in layer meta and the validation sheet. *Invalidated by:* network recovery — a healthy-network rebuild re-fetches at 1/3 arc-second.

## D-023 (2026-11-12, Tier B pilot 4) — Portal-mount drop escalation: stage in /tmp, settle-and-heal, cursed-path escape
The /mnt drop behavior (D-019) escalated: `nhd_waterbodies_20019.geojson` vanished after FOUR consecutive verified recopies, ~10 s apart each — the portal's async delete queue appears to stick to a path; and `eia860_plants_20019.csv` flip-flopped (present at fetcher start → gone at EXPECT seconds later → present again next attempt), which defeated fetch_grid's early-exit skip (stale `exists()`). ## D-024 (2026-11-12, Tier B pilot 5) — DEM window-clipping bug cropped ALL pilot terrains; coverage now validated
Pilot 5's closeup render showed the terrain mesh ending mid-county. Root cause in `fetch_terrain.py read_windows`: `from_bounds(buffered_bbox, tile_transform)` yields windows that overhang partially-overlapping 1° tiles; rasterio `read(boundless=False)` silently CLIPS such windows, but `window_transform()` was taken from the UNCLIPPED window, so clipped arrays were mis-anchored at the bbox corner and the mosaic collapsed to the last fully-inside tile. Every pilot DEM was cropped where the buffer crosses a tile edge (lat/lon coverage: Thurston 84%/99.7%, Teton 92%/97%, McDowell 100%/93%, Chautauqua 85%/86%, Wilcox 59%/78%). Elevation min/max validation passed throughout — **statistics cannot catch a spatial crop**; render review did. Fix: intersect each window with the tile extent before reading and take the transform from the clipped window. `validate_county.py` gains a "terrain coverage of county polygon ≥99%" check (bbox coverage AND rasterized valid-cell coverage). All five pilot terrains were refetched (full 1/3″ TNM quality — the egress throttle had lifted, so McDowell upgrades from the 30 m D-021 fallback) and all five stages rebuilt. **Rule: any world layer must validate spatial coverage, not just value statistics.**

## D-028 (2026-11-15, national rollout step 1) — OSM from Geofabrik state extracts, clipped locally; Overpass demoted to fallback
Every public Overpass mirror failed for most of 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-12 (D-018, D-022), and a 3,144-county rollout would put roughly 31,000 heavy queries on volunteer-run servers. `fetch_osm.py` now downloads each Geofabrik U.S. state extract once into the shared cache (`$RURALSIM_CACHE`, md5-verified against Geofabrik's `.md5`), picks every extract whose outline touches the county's buffered bbox (so border features are kept), and clips with pyosmium on the build machine. `--state XX` clips every 2024-vintage county of a state in one pass per extract, which is how the national run should call it. Selection uses the same keys and the same unanchored value regexes as the Overpass queries, and the GeoJSON schema is unchanged (`osm_type`, `osm_id`, tags; closed ways are Polygons only when tagged building or area=yes; relations are MultiPolygons of outer rings), so `build_stage.py` and the harness read the output unmodified. Documented difference: Overpass also returns a relation when only a member node is in the bbox; the clipper keeps a relation when an outer member way touches it — the only relations the GeoJSON keeps are matched identically. Features present in two extracts are de-duplicated by OSM type and id. The extract URL, md5 and replication timestamp go into `layers_raw/osm/_meta.json`; `build_stage.py` cites the snapshot for non-pilot counties and leaves the pilots' Overpass label byte-stable. The old fetcher survives unchanged as `fetch_osm_overpass.py` (`fetch_osm.py --source overpass`). Offline semantics test: `tests/test_fetch_osm.py`; pilot parity check: `tools/compare_osm.py <fips>`. **Parity (2026-11-15, Thurston 31173, Iowa + Nebraska extracts):** 9 of 10 themes ≥98.8% shared OSM ids (buildings, schools, healthcare, power, places, food_shops 100%; roads 98.8%; bridges 99.6%). `water` flagged at 8 vs 9: the extra feature is node 14066337249 (`man_made=water_tower`, version 1, created 2026-08-01), inside the bbox and absent from the 2026-11-10 Overpass answer — a mirror omission (cf. D-018), not a clipper error. The first run also exposed that U.S. states cannot be selected from the Geofabrik index by `parent`; they are now matched by extract URL. Remaining four pilots to be checked in the canary run. *Invalidated by:* a parity failure on another pilot that OSM edits between the two retrieval dates do not explain.

## D-030 (2026-11-15, national rollout step 1) — Water systems placed by town or ZIP point; validation counts active systems only
Every pilot flagged "water systems placed" (Thurston 9 of 37, McDowell 21 of 183) for two reasons. The validation denominator counted every SDWIS row, inactive and closed systems included, while the stage only places active ones; and placement matched a single served-town string exactly against OSM place nodes, so multi-town values ("GEORGIANA,PINE APPLE"), annotations ("NORTHFORK (RR NAME NORTH FORK)") and blank town fields never matched. New `place_water_systems.py` (run by `fetch_water.py`) writes `sdwis_locations_<fips>.csv` for active systems: split and normalise the served towns, match OSM places, then Census Gazetteer 2024 places in the same state whose point is inside the county's buffered bbox; for systems with no town, look up city and ZIP once in EPA's WATER_SYSTEM table (contact fields are never stored), and fall back to the Census ZCTA point for the ZIP. A WATER_SYSTEM city or ZIP is a mailing address and is accepted only inside the bbox. Positions are served-town or ZIP points, tagged derived. `build_stage.py` uses the file when present (pilots without it keep the old logic) and `validate_county.py` compares against active systems. Result on the pilots' committed tables, active systems placed: Wilcox 5/6, Teton 21/26, Chautauqua 10/10, Thurston 9/9, McDowell 23/24. The remaining six have no matchable town or ZIP inside the county. Offline tests: `tests/test_climate_water.py`.

Planetary Computer is a maintained service, not a volunteer mirror, and the national CDL raster is several GB per year; revisit if Planetary Computer fails in the canary run. ## D-100 (2026-11-21, national rollout step 2) — AI-infrastructure engine: spec v0.1 before code
Step 2 replaces EXP-0007's parametric accounting model (`eng_exp0007_datacenter`) with a screening engine of five modules — power flow, rate impact, cooling water, fiber, flood — specified in `specs/ai_infra_engine.yaml` before any engine code is written. The spec rests on an inventory of three published counties (`specs/ai_infra_layer_inventory.md`: Wilcox AL 01131, Licking OH 39089, Pottawattamie IA 19155). What the inventory found, misses first: no distribution feeders in any county (OSM has pole points but almost no lines joining them); the EIA-860 file has no capacity, because `fetch_grid.py` reads only the Plant sheet; the EIA-861 file has no co-op flag and no rates, despite the docstring; no retail territory polygons, so a county's 4–8 utilities cannot be assigned to a point; no humidity in any climate file; no streamflow in NHD; no capacity or population served in SDWIS. OSM transmission is usable: 92–100% of in-county line km and 93–100% of substations carry a voltage tag. Choices: (1) the grid is modelled on observed OSM topology with ratings and impedances drawn by voltage class from the Texas A&M ACTIVSg synthetic cases, used as parameter distributions only, never as topology; NREL SMART-DS feeder statistics are used only if a load falls in the distribution class, after its rural coverage is checked. (2) Because the serving utility is unknown, rate impact runs for every EIA-861 utility in the county and reports the range. (3) Every assumed input is sampled per seed — EXP-0007 v0 ignored the seed, so its 20 seeds returned one value (+16.7% with on-site generation, −23.8% grid-only) with a zero-width interval. (4) Flood stays blocked under D-013; a terrain-derived screen is possible from published layers but is not computed unless D-013 is superseded. (5) Licking and Pottawattamie are metro (RUCC 1 and 2) and on step 3's back-test candidate list: no engine parameter may be tuned on any candidate county, and the runner refuses a back-test site until step 3's pre-registration is merged. Nine layer requests (LR-1 to LR-9) go to the step-1 chat; none changes the build pipeline here. Every result is SPECULATIVE. *Invalidated by:* a public source of feeder topology or retail territories, or a layer request that cannot be met (the dependent module stays blocked).

## D-106 (2026-11-22, rollout step 2) — Cooling-water module; EXP-0012
`tools/engines/ai_infra/cooling_water.py` implements the spec's third module on LR-3 to LR-6 (D-054, D-056). Daily water over 30 years of NASA POWER weather at the county centroid. **Evaporative:** make-up = evaporation + blowdown; evaporation is the latent share of rejected heat (IT × PUE) over 0.681 kWh/kg; the latent share rises from 0.60 to 0.90 as daily-mean wet-bulb rises from 0 to 25 °C (±0.05); cycles of concentration 3–6. **Adiabatic hybrid:** evaporative pre-cooling only on days whose daily-mean dry bulb reaches a set point of 18–24 °C, evaporating 20–50% of heat on those days. **Dry:** no water; PUE +0.05–0.20 on days above 22–27 °C daily mean, reported but not yet fed into power flow. All ranges are assumed and sampled per seed (`params/assumed.yaml`, `cooling:`). Evaporative WUE comes out at 1.8–2.0 L per IT kWh, against a U.S. data-center average of about 1.8 (Shehabi et al. 2016), which is an outside anchor, not a fit. **Comparisons:** county 2015 public-supply and total fresh withdrawals (USGS); active community water systems serving the county (SDWIS, via the county's published system list); dry-season low flow (`p10_min_cfs`) and mean flow at a source gauge. **Source rule:** the largest-drainage gauge with low-flow statistics within 50 km of the site, because the nearest gauge is often a creek a large user would not draw from (in Wilcox, Pine Barren Creek at 19 cfs, against the Alabama River at Claiborne at 4,150 cfs); the nearest is reported, unscored. Flow is taken at the gauge (scale factor 1.0): scaling to the site needs the site's drainage area, which needs NHDPlus. **Card EXP-0012, bars set before the run:** p50 peak-day withdrawal ≤ 10% of source low flow; p50 annual withdrawal ≤ 10% of county public supply. Screening conventions, assumed. ## D-400 (2026-11-22, governance) — Pending public decision: calibrated work is published in full, or not taken
**Trigger.** A pending public decision exists from the first of these until final action, and re-opens on amendment or appeal: (1) a proposal is filed with, or on an agenda of, a public body that will vote, hold a hearing, or issue a permit, license, certificate, rezoning, tax abatement or development agreement, water or air permit, interconnection approval or contract approval for it; (2) a public body has adopted, or is studying, a moratorium, pause or suspension covering the proposal's type. *Public body* means the governing body or agency of a state, county, city, special district (hospital, water, groundwater, school or utility) or state regulatory commission, and federal agencies. Tribal decisions follow D-420. **Bears on:** models the proposal, an alternative to it (including no build), or a system element the decision affects, or is done for a party to the decision. **Calibrated work:** any run using non-public data (Tier D partner calibration, paid calibrated study, custom experiment card on client data, private-workspace run). **Published in full:** every bearing run including misses and aborted runs; the pre-registered method, pass bar and grade; what isn't modelled; which inputs were private and what calibrating on them changed (direction and size); both disclosure lines; the resident view once D-402 is adopted. "In full" never includes the private data, member or meter records, or detailed feeder layouts; each publication says an outside reader cannot rerun it without the partner's data. **Timing:** published on ruralsim.io and delivered to the public body's clerk no later than 10 business days before the vote or hearing, or 5 business days after completion, whichever is first; partner review is capped at 5 business days and never moves the deadline. Contested commission cases: file on the docket as public comment or don't deliver; never privately to commissioners or staff. **Springing clause:** if a trigger arises after work starts or finishes, prior bearing work falls under this rule; the partner consents to publication or the work stops, and the ledger records a stop notice (date, pre-registered method, "stopped at a pending public decision", no results, no partner name). **Ledger stub, always:** every calibrated run gets a public ledger record within 30 days (date, county, pre-registered method and pass bar, pass or fail), pending decision or not. **Intake declaration:** the partner or client declares any known pending decision at intake and within 5 business days of learning of one; RuralSim also checks agendas at intake and monthly; a false declaration is a material breach. Supersedes D-302 where a decision is pending; broadens D-305 to any public body; extends Revenue Rule 3 to private clients when a decision is pending. Does not change grading, the disclosure lines, the pledge, the internal screen, data privacy or name consent. Source: `governance/TOGETHER_D-ENTRY_DRAFTS.md` Part B (option A). Redlines to `step4/PARTNER_OFFER.md` and `step4/DATA_SHARING_TERM_SHEET.md` per `governance/TOGETHER_REDLINES.md`; attorney review of the springing clause before any signature. *Invalidated by:* evidence that full publication makes the public decision worse (e.g. a body excludes the analysis from the record); revisit after the first three triggered engagements.

## D-401 (2026-11-22, governance) — Hospital service lines: method, grade and plain summary published by default; data stays private
**Service line:** inpatient beds, obstetrics/labor and delivery, emergency department, surgery, swing beds, ambulance/EMS, behavioral health, a rural health clinic, dialysis, or another service the hospital names. **Reduction:** closing it, ending it at one site, cutting hours or days, moving it out of county, or converting to a Rural Emergency Hospital. **Trigger (H1 with backstop):** a reduction is on a board agenda, in a board-commissioned study, or in a filing (REH conversion, state notice); if no board action follows, the summary is published 12 months after delivery anyway. **Published:** the pre-registered method and pass bar, the grade, what isn't modelled, the misses, and a plain-language summary in English and Spanish to the resident-view standard. Effects on residents (travel time, share of synthetic households beyond 30/60 minutes, EMS transport time, the worst-off 10% check) are published in full; financial effects as direction and range, with exact figures only from the public Medicare cost report. **Private:** monthly volumes, payer mix, staffing, contracts, patient origin below county. **Small numbers:** no count from 1 to 10 is shown ("fewer than 11", per CMS cell-size suppression); derived figures shown as ranges; no month-level series for a line under 11 events a month. **Timing:** at least 10 business days before any board vote on the reduction, or 30 days after delivery, whichever is first. Public hospitals (district, county, city) are public bodies under Revenue Rule 3 and D-400; the stricter rule governs. Revenue Rule 3's example is corrected to "private clients, such as privately owned hospitals"; ownership is confirmed at intake. Compatible with the de-identified/aggregate-data rule because of the small-numbers clause. Source: `governance/TOGETHER_D-ENTRY_DRAFTS.md` Part D. *Invalidated by:* a HIPAA BAA era (published summaries must then also meet 45 CFR §164.514(b)).

## D-420 (2026-11-22, governance) — Tribal lands: public geography only; screens only at a nation's request
Adopted as drafted in `governance/D-420_TRIBAL_LANDS_DRAFT.md`, Rules 1–6. In short: tribal land (Census TIGER AIANNH: CLASSFP D0, D2, D3, D5, D8, and Oklahoma Tribal Statistical Areas D6, including fee land inside reservation boundaries) appears only as public geography, labelled "jurisdiction of the [Nation]". No experiment, screen, siting, synthetic population, score or index (including the RQLI), ranking, tribal-land subtotal or narrative claim is produced about tribal land or its people unless that nation's government requests it; a request from anyone else, including the county, is not enough. In partly tribal counties, screens mask tribal land from candidate sites and from the modelled population and say so.
