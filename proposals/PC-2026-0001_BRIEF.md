## The question
What would a data center on the ~300-acre site proposed in north Hillsboro mean for Hill County's grid, power bills and water, compared with no build? The Hill County Commissioners Court voted on 2026-05-12 on a one-year pause on new data centers in unincorporated areas.

**Who asked:** nobody (dry run). The site is one RuralSim picked to stand in for the location, an H3 cell `8826cb7233fffff` in north Hillsboro (location approximate). It is not the parcel. No size was public, so the two build options are RuralSim's assumptions: 100 MW and 300 MW of IT load.

**Jurisdiction note:** the one project named in public by the cutoff (Provident Data Centers, ~300 acres) is reported to be inside Hillsboro city limits. A county pause on unincorporated areas would not reach it. The grid, bill and water effects below land on the county either way.

## Read this first
1. **MISS:** At 100 MW, the screen connects the site at the nearest 138 kV substation (3.5 km away). Lines there overload in 3 of 20 runs, and the bar was at most 2 of 20.
2. **MISS:** With evaporative cooling, annual water draw would be about **33%** of the county's public water supply at 100 MW and about **99%** at 300 MW. The bar was at most 10%.
3. **CAN'T TELL:** Bills for the lowest-income households, and so the worst-off rule. The site would be 1.4–6.6 times the annual sales of each nearby co-op, which is beyond the rate model's range. Oncor and TNMP sell no power directly, so their bills can't be modelled.

**Why 300 MW passes the line test when 100 MW fails:** a larger load connects at a higher voltage. The 300 MW option ties into a 345 kV substation 16.8 km away, which needs a new high-voltage line. Who pays for that line, and what it costs, is not modelled. A bigger build is not shown to be safer.

## What the results rest on
- **Site:** H3 r8 cell `8826cb7233fffff`, picked by RuralSim to stand in for north Hillsboro. Not the parcel.
- **Load:** no MW figure was public. 100 and 300 MW are dry-run assumptions, about 1 MW per acre at the top.
- **Cooling:** cooling was not public, so all three types were run. The water result moves with cooling more than with anything else.
- **Water baseline:** county public supply of 2.97 Mgal/d (USGS 2015). The gauge is Brazos River near Aquilla (08093100), with a low flow (p10_min) of 22 cfs.
- **Grid:** observed OpenStreetMap lines (69 kV: 153 km; 138 kV: 166 km; 345 kV: 136 km in the county). Ratings and impedances are assumed by voltage class.
- **Data:** EIA-861 2024 and county snapshot `20261117T032935Z-f8fe365`. Both are later than 2026-05-12. The geography shows no building on the site, so nothing needed masking.

## Questions the public record can't answer
1. To the applicant: what IT load (MW) and what cooling type, and is there a phase schedule?
2. To the serving utility (Oncor, or the co-op for the site): which substation and voltage will serve the site, and who pays for any new line?
3. To the city and the applicant: what is the water source, and what is the annual and peak-day draw?
4. To the utility and the Public Utility Commission of Texas: how are the costs of a load this size kept off residential bills?
5. To the applicant and the appraisal district: the eight fiscal questions below.

## Fiscal facts (observed; no dollar figure, D-430)
- **Business personal property tax:** Texas taxes business personal property, including servers, unless an exemption applies (Tax Code §11.01, §22.01).
- **State sales-tax exemptions:** §151.359 (qualifying data center) and §151.3595 (large data center project). Local sales taxes are not exempt.
- **Local agreements:** none found in the public registries, which is not evidence that none exist.
- **Taxing-unit rates and levies:** not in RuralSim yet (LR-14).

**The fixed questions to demand:**
1. What share of the investment is real property, and what share is equipment?
2. What depreciation schedule applies to the equipment, and what taxable value is expected in years 1, 5 and 10?
3. Which exemptions and abatements are requested, and for each, which taxing unit gives up revenue, how much and for how long?
4. What performance terms (jobs, investment, clawback) come with each incentive, and who checks them?
5. What does each taxing unit gain or lose, especially school districts after any state school-finance offset?
6. What new public costs follow: roads, water and wastewater, EMS and fire, and inspection staff?
7. Is a PILOT, development agreement or community benefit agreement available, and what would it secure?
8. How does long-term revenue compare with long-term service and infrastructure costs?
